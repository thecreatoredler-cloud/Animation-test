# Audio guide

> **Current method (pilot-02 onward): `scripts/foley.py` event-driven modal Foley.** The cue-based `audio_tools.py` (below) is kept for v1 reproducibility only. Its noise beds, whooshes and sub-bass "weight" hits were diagnosed as the reason v1 sounded synthetic (projects/pilot-02-padlock/diagnosis/DIAGNOSIS_v1.md).

## Foley workflow (use this)
1. In the shot's `build()`, call `ev(t, type, strength=..., until=..., speed=[...])` at every **physical contact** the animation creates. Compute the time from the same function that moves the part (frame accuracy, exact sync). They're written to `build/events_<shot>.json`.
2. In `project.json["foley"]["recipes"]`, map each event type to a recipe:
   - `impact`: one contact exciting coupled objects (`objects` from `foley.OBJECTS`; `hardness_ms` = contact duration, shorter is brighter).
   - `friction`: sliding contact, with a Poisson micro-impact rate following `speed`.
   - `file`: a licensed recording (record its licence).
   - `mute`: for events that aren't sounds (visual devices, alignment beats).
   Set relative `gain_db` per recipe. Don't normalise each sound.
3. `space_timeline`: `close` (dry) for macro and close-up, `medium`/`wide` add short early reflections and a small tail.
4. Master: one gain to `target_lufs`, true-peak ceiling −1 dBTP. Check `limiter_max_gr_db` in the log: over about 4 dB means the climax is too far above the rest.
5. Verify with `scripts/audio_analysis.py`: every intended click detected as an event, no noise beds, no energy below 100 Hz unless a large object makes it. **Then a human listens.**

Object modes (`foley.OBJECTS`) are estimates. Add new objects from their size and material (beam formula: f_n = (β_n²/2π)·√(EI/ρA)/L²) and short T60s for held or clamped parts.

## Legacy (v1, superseded; don't use for new videos)
The rules below produced v1's synthetic sound. In particular, the 50–80 Hz "weight" layer is wrong for small objects. They're kept only so pilot-01 can be reproduced.

Tool: `scripts/audio_tools.py` (synthesised SFX and the mix), called by `video_assembler.py`. Sounds are generated by code, so they are original and licence-free. A cue can use a licensed file instead (`"file": ...`); record it in `asset_licenses.md`.

## The four layers (tutorial T6)
1. **Bed**: `room` (interiors) or `wind` (exteriors) at −40 to −46 dB, for the whole video. Never dead digital silence.
2. **Weight**: a low thump (`weight`, 50–80 Hz) under every big moment.
3. **Main**: the sound of the action (`click`, `scrape`, `ting`, `whoosh`, or a file).
4. **Detail**: a high, short layer (`tick`) a few ms after the main sound.

`impact` already stacks weight, click and tick. Use it for locks clicking, objects landing and hits.

## Sound follows the camera
Each cue has `space`:
| space | Use when the shot is | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `close` | Macro or close-up | 6 % reverb, full top end: dry and detailed |
| `medium` | Medium shot | 15 % reverb, top cut at 12 kHz |
| `wide` | Wide or establishing | 30 % reverb, 1.1 s tail, top cut at 7 kHz: roomy and dull |

## Sync
- Put cues at the **exact frame time** of the visual event (frame/30). When motion is procedural, compute cue times from the same function. The pilot's pin clicks came from `pin_bottom()` peak detection, which gave 12 click times.
- A whoosh starts 2–4 frames before a fast camera move or cut and peaks on it.
- A ringing tail (`ting`) can overlap the next beat; clicks should not.
- Master: normalise the loudest hit to −3 dBFS. With narration, keep SFX peaks about 6 dB under the voice.

## Narration
There is no offline text-to-speech model in this environment (speech-model hosts are blocked). Options in order:
1. The owner records the narration, and `project.json["narration"]` points to the WAV.
2. A free TTS run on the owner's side, then dropped in.
3. Captions-only (the pilot).
