# Audio guide

Tool: `scripts/audio_tools.py` (synthesised SFX and the mix), called by `video_assembler.py`. Sounds are generated by code, so they are original and licence-free. A cue can use a licensed file instead (`"file": ...`); record it in `asset_licenses.md`.

## The four layers (tutorial T6)
1. **Bed**: `room` (interiors) or `wind` (exteriors) at −40 to −46 dB, for the whole video. Never dead digital silence.
2. **Weight**: a low thump (`weight`, 50–80 Hz) under every big moment.
3. **Main**: the sound of the action (`click`, `scrape`, `ting`, `whoosh`, or a file).
4. **Detail**: a high, short layer (`tick`) a few ms after the main sound.

`impact` already stacks weight, click and tick. Use it for locks clicking, objects landing and hits.

## Sound follows the camera
Each cue has `space`:
| space | Use when the shot is | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `close` | Macro or close-up | 6 % reverb, full top end: dry and detailed |
| `medium` | Medium shot | 15 % reverb, top cut at 12 kHz |
| `wide` | Wide or establishing | 30 % reverb, 1.1 s tail, top cut at 7 kHz: roomy and dull |

## Sync
- Put cues at the **exact frame time** of the visual event (frame/30). When motion is procedural, compute cue times from the same function. The pilot's pin clicks came from `pin_bottom()` peak detection, which gave 12 click times.
- A whoosh starts 2–4 frames before a fast camera move or cut and peaks on it.
- A ringing tail (`ting`) can overlap the next beat; clicks should not.
- Master: normalise the loudest hit to −3 dBFS. With narration, keep SFX peaks about 6 dB under the voice.

## Narration
There is no offline text-to-speech model in this environment (speech-model hosts are blocked). Options in order:
1. The owner records the narration, and `project.json["narration"]` points to the WAV.
2. A free TTS run on the owner's side, then dropped in.
3. Captions-only (the pilot).
